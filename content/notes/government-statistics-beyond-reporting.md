Statistics in public institutions often begin as a reporting obligation. A table is requested, a template is filled, and a number is submitted.

That work matters, but it is not enough. The deeper value of statistics is that it helps an institution notice what is changing, what is unclear, what is underperforming, and what needs a better question.

Good public data systems should support reporting, planning, budgeting, monitoring, and learning. They should make it easier for people to compare, interpret, and act.

The shift is small but important: from producing numbers because they are required, to building evidence that helps decisions become more thoughtful.
